Scope of Work — Sage 300-side write-back

  • The ERP-side write-back connector is built by the entity. No Sage 300 SDK/Business Logic Object development or crystal-report print change on the Sage 300 side is part of the current proposal. What the Hub delivers ends at its interface.
  • The Sage 300 team collects the data and writes the record. They call the Hub interface, take the UUID, status, acknowledgement number, Peppol reference and QR information, and set the Optional Fields on this invoice on their own release cycle.
  • Inbound supplier invoices are created as drafts. When the Hub passes a supplier invoice to Sage 300, the entity's team creates it as an unposted A/P batch entry for a person to review. Nothing is posted automatically.
  • If Ras Al Hamra Group wants us to build it, it is quoted separately. Scope, effort, timeline and cost for the Sage 300-side work are estimated as a separate engagement.

Everything on this screen uses sample data to show what the finished result would look like on the Sage 300 record. It is not a working integration.

The end of the walkthrough

The loop is closed.

  1. The invoice left Sage 300 on its own, as a normal O/E invoice.
  2. It was mapped, built as UBL 2.1, validated against the Oman PINT-OM rules and archived.
  3. The Hub transmitted it to the accredited service provider, which reported it to the Oman Tax Authority and delivered it to the buyer.
  4. Its UUID, status, acknowledgement, Peppol reference and QR are ready to be collected from the Hub and shown on this invoice — the same screen the finance team already uses.

Step 4 is the entity’s Sage 300 team’s work, not the Hub’s. The Hub’s delivery ends where it publishes the data. Building the Sage 300-side write-back connector is a separately scoped, separately costed piece of work.
