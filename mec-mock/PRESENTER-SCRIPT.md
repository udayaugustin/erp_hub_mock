# Presenter script — MEC × Fawtara (≈12 minutes)

**Act I — the pipe.** Sign in. On the dashboard, point at the six streams: four as seller, two as
buyer. Two are amber — the treatments we bring to the OTA.

**Act II — a milestone.** In the Enterprise ERP, Ghaba North certifies Milestone 4 on contract
C-2025-118; MEC-INV-2026-00382 is posted (OMR 198,400 net, including variation VO-03; OMR 188,790
payable on the e-invoice after the advance already paid). The Mapping Studio shows how MEC's ERP
fields point at PINT-OM — an analyst's choice, no code. In the Hub it goes through nine stages,
and the document inspector shows the XML validated before sending. The ASP exchange shows three
acknowledgements coming back on their own timelines. The ASP validates; the OTA does not clear or
reject it.

**Act III — manhours and corrections.** July timesheets for the Duqm owner's-engineer contract:
2,496 approved hours across eight disciplines become one invoice. Hours a client rejects after
invoicing go out as a credit note; agreed escalation as a debit note — each referencing the original.

**Act IV — the open questions.** Export of services to a Saudi client at 0%, and why branch
recharges stay out of the pipe. Retention money and advances: VAT on the full value now, the advance
taxed once, retention a payment term and not on the e-invoice. All tagged "to confirm with OTA".

**Act V — buyer side and the record.** Subcontractor e-invoices land as drafts coded to the
project; a Norwegian software licence arrives as a PDF and is self-accounted. Reports by stream
and by project, who does what (MEC's side of the line and Fawtara's), why Fawtara, and finally the UUID and QR back on the original ERP invoice.
